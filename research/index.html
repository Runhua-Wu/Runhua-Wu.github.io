Queueing systems with customer abandonments

Research perspective

Queueing systems with customer abandonments, also called queues with impatient customers, arise in many real-world service environments, including call centers, healthcare systems, and perishable-inventory models. In these systems, customers may leave before receiving service when their waiting times exceed their tolerance levels. This behavior can significantly affect system performance through lost revenue, reduced service efficiency, and lower customer satisfaction. Analyzing and optimizing such systems is therefore both practically important and mathematically rich.

Because abandonment interacts directly with congestion, control decisions can influence both waiting times and the likelihood that customers leave. My work seeks policy structures that are mathematically exact, interpretable, and computationally useful.

Current projects span admission control, service-rate control, and scheduling. See Publications for papers and preprints.
